# Verified Research Papers

This collection contains verified scholarly literature related to AI-powered literature discovery, scientific paper recommendation, citation recommendation, scholarly search, information retrieval, research automation, and scientific-language processing. Each paper has been checked for bibliographic information and linked to a DOI, publisher page, or authoritative scholarly source.

## Survey and Review Papers

### 1. Scientific Paper Recommendation Systems: A Literature Review of Recent Publications

Christin Katharina Kreutz, Ralf Schenkel, 2022, *International Journal on Digital Libraries*, 23(4), 335–369.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00799-022-00339-w

A comprehensive review of scientific paper recommendation systems, including recommendation methods, datasets, evaluation approaches, and open challenges.

### 2. Scholarly Recommendation Systems: A Literature Survey

Zitong Zhang, Braja Gopal Patra, Ashraf Yaseen, Jie Zhu, Rachit Sabharwal, Kirk Roberts, Tru Cao, Hulin Wu, 2023, *Knowledge and Information Systems*, 65, 4433–4478.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10115-023-01901-x

Reviews scholarly recommendation systems covering literature, collaborators, conferences, journals, datasets, and grants.

### 3. Scientific Paper Recommendation: A Survey

Xiaomei Bai, Mengyang Wang, Ivan Lee, Zhuo Yang, Xiangjie Kong, Feng Xia, 2020, *arXiv*.
Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13538

Surveys content-based, collaborative-filtering, graph-based, and hybrid approaches for scientific paper recommendation.

### 4. An Anatomization of Research Paper Recommender System: Overview, Approaches and Challenges

Ritu Sharma, Dinesh Gopalani, Yogesh Meena, 2023, *Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence*, 118, 105641.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engappai.2022.105641

Provides an extensive analysis of research-paper recommender systems, including content-based, collaborative, link-based, co-occurrence, global-relevance, and hybrid approaches.

### 5. Citation Recommendation: Approaches and Datasets

Michael Färber, Adam Jatowt, 2020, *International Journal on Digital Libraries*, 21, 375–405.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00799-020-00288-2

Reviews automatic citation-recommendation approaches, datasets, evaluation methods, and challenges in recommending scholarly citations.

### 6. A Review of Citation Recommendation: From Textual Content to Enriched Context

Shutian Ma, Chengzhi Zhang, Xiaozhong Liu, 2020, *Scientometrics*, 122(3), 1445–1472.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-019-03336-0

Examines the development of citation recommendation from text-based methods toward approaches incorporating citation networks, authorship, and contextual information.

### 7. A Systematic Review of Citation Recommendation Over the Past Two Decades

Yicong Liang, Lap-Kei Lee, 2023, *International Journal on Semantic Web and Information Systems*, 19(1).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.4018/IJSWIS.324071

Systematically reviews citation-recommendation models, datasets, evaluation methods, and major developments over two decades.

## Foundational Information Retrieval and Scholarly Discovery

### 8. Citation Indexes for Science: A New Dimension in Documentation Through Association of Ideas

Eugene Garfield, 1955, *Science*, 122(3159), 108–111.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.122.3159.108

Introduced citation indexing as a mechanism for connecting scientific literature and enabling discovery through citation relationships.

### 9. Co-Citation in the Scientific Literature: A New Measure of the Relationship Between Two Documents

Henry Small, 1973, *Journal of the American Society for Information Science*, 24(4), 265–269.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.4630240406

Introduced co-citation analysis as a method for identifying relationships and clusters within scientific literature.

### 10. CiteSeer: An Automatic Citation Indexing System

C. Lee Giles, Kurt D. Bollacker, Steve Lawrence, 1998, *Proceedings of the Third ACM Conference on Digital Libraries*, 89–98.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/276675.276685

Describes an early automated citation-indexing system that enabled computer-assisted discovery and navigation of scientific literature.

### 11. Term-Weighting Approaches in Automatic Text Retrieval

Gerard Salton, Christopher Buckley, 1988, *Information Processing & Management*, 24(5), 513–523.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0306-4573(88)90021-0

Provides foundational research on term weighting and document ranking for automatic information retrieval.

### 12. A Statistical Interpretation of Term Specificity and Its Application in Retrieval

Karen Spärck Jones, 1972, *Journal of Documentation*, 28(1), 11–21.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1108/eb026526

Introduces a statistical interpretation of term specificity that became an important foundation for information-retrieval weighting methods.

### 13. The Probabilistic Relevance Framework: BM25 and Beyond

Stephen Robertson, Hugo Zaragoza, 2009, *Foundations and Trends in Information Retrieval*, 4(1–2), 1–174.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1561/1500000019

Provides a detailed treatment of the probabilistic relevance framework and BM25, a foundational ranking method in information retrieval.
